# BAROS Preliminary Translational Workshare and Budget Model

> Planning model only. Not a commitment, subcontract, sponsored-research agreement, IP agreement, or final budget. All allocations remain subject to sponsor rules, institutional indirect-cost policies, negotiated scope, and actual effort.

## Funding principle

Funding is apportioned according to scientifically necessary workshare, facilities, personnel, data stewardship, regulatory burden, and sponsor requirements. No payment is contingent on a favorable validation result. Academic/clinical investigators must retain independence to report failures or negative results.

For NIH PAR-25-337, the current NOFO permits up to $499,000 in direct costs per year for up to five years and states that investigators may set effort levels and apportion budget according to project requirements.

## Base planning scenario

A reasonable starting allocation for discussion is **40% industrial / BAROS engineering and 60% academic medical-physics / clinical validation**, before institutional indirect costs. This is not a proposed entitlement; it reflects the expectation that independent physical measurements, clinical data operations, statistics, medical-physics time, workflow studies, and institutional/regulatory execution may consume the larger share of translational-validation resources.

### Aim-level planning split

| Work package | BAROS / industrial | Academic / clinical | Rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Aim 1A — frozen build, provenance, deployment tooling | 75% | 25% | Engineering-heavy |
| Aim 1B — commissioning, measurement, end-to-end dosimetry | 20% | 80% | Independent medical-physics work must dominate |
| Aim 2 — retrospective + shadow-mode validation | 25% | 75% | Clinical data, planning comparison, adjudication, statistics |
| Aim 3 — regulated prospective study preparation/execution | 20% | 80% | Clinical/institutional burden dominates |
| Cross-cutting — regulatory strategy, data management, publication | 45% | 55% | Joint translational responsibility |

The weighted total should be recomputed from actual personnel effort and costs once the indication, number of sites, sample size, equipment needs, and study phases are locked.

## Illustrative annual direct-cost envelope

If a PAR-25-337 application requested the current maximum of $499,000 direct costs/year, a **40/60 planning split** would correspond approximately to:

- BAROS / industrial direct-cost share: **$199,600/year**
- Academic / clinical direct-cost share: **$299,400/year**

These figures are arithmetic illustrations only; they are not proposed final amounts and do not include negotiated indirect-cost treatment.

## BAROS / industrial cost categories

- software engineering and verification;
- DICOM/TPS interoperability engineering;
- reproducibility, audit, cybersecurity, configuration and release control;
- deployment/support tooling for frozen research versions;
- defect remediation from independent validation findings;
- regulatory engineering documentation;
- commercialization and sustainability planning only where sponsor-allowable.

## Academic / clinical cost categories

- medical physicist effort;
- radiation oncologist / clinical investigator effort;
- dosimetry and treatment-planning personnel;
- phantom, detector, machine and measurement time;
- clinical data extraction/de-identification and data management;
- biostatistics;
- research coordination;
- IRB/regulatory administration where allowable;
- retrospective, shadow-mode and prospective study execution;
- independent adjudication and publication.

## Governance and IP separation

Sponsored-project budget allocation is separate from IP ownership, inventorship, licensing, equity, commercialization rights, publication rights, and future revenue. Those matters must be addressed through appropriate institutional/company agreements based on actual background IP and new contributions.

No collaborator is asked to provide a favorable result in exchange for funding. Validation evidence remains valid only if negative findings can be reported and progression can be stopped.

## Negotiation position

The recommended opening position is:

1. Treat scientific workshare as the basis for budget distribution.
2. Give the academic validation partner sufficient funded independence to perform measurements and analysis without dependence on BAROS conclusions.
3. Preserve BAROS background IP while recognizing collaborator inventorship/new IP according to actual contribution and applicable law/policy.
4. Use milestone-based continuation: intended-use lock -> measured-dose gate -> retrospective gate -> shadow-mode gate -> regulated prospective gate.
5. Rebalance budget by phase as academic/clinical workload rises or falls.

## Required inputs before final budget

- applicant/prime organization;
- final academic partner and participating departments;
- PI/multi-PI structure;
- intended-use indication;
- participating treatment platforms/TPS products;
- number of sites;
- study sample sizes and statistical design;
- personnel effort and institutional salary/fringe assumptions;
- indirect-cost rates;
- equipment/supplies/service needs;
- subaward structure;
- data/IRB/regulatory requirements;
- sponsor-specific allowability rules.
