# Worldshepherd Assurance Composite Benchmark v0.1

**Status:** `BENCHMARK PROTOCOL / NO SUPERIORITY CLAIM YET`

## Purpose

Define a reproducible benchmark for whether a frozen Worldshepherd candidate provides a stronger **integrated assurance envelope** than assembling leading public tools independently.

The benchmark is deliberately harder than comparing Worldshepherd with one product. The comparison set is a **best-of-breed composite** drawn from public capabilities of mature projects and services such as:

- SLSA / in-toto — provenance and attestations;
- Sigstore / Cosign — signing and verification;
- GitHub Artifact Attestations — CI-native build provenance and offline verification;
- Witness — provenance collection, verification, policy and air-gap-oriented workflows;
- Open Policy Agent (OPA) — distributed policy decision enforcement and decision logs;
- GUAC — software-supply-chain evidence graph / SBOM / provenance / vulnerability relationships;
- OCSF — normalized security-event semantics where relevant;
- OpenSSF Scorecard and related ecosystem evidence — public project-security signals.

Worldshepherd should reuse compatible standards rather than claim that equivalent internal formats are inherently superior.

## Non-negotiable comparison rule

A competitor capability is scored only from a reproducible public artifact, official documentation, executable release, or attributable external result. **Unknown is not zero.** Unknown means `NOT_EVALUATED` and is excluded from comparative arithmetic until tested.

Worldshepherd receives the same treatment. Internal architecture prose alone cannot score as implemented behavior.

No document generated by Worldshepherd may state `best`, `superior`, `market-leading`, `beats`, `outperforms`, or equivalent language unless a frozen benchmark run supports that exact scope and the referenced competitors were tested under the same protocol.

## Scoring model

Each benchmark dimension receives four sub-scores:

| Sub-score | Range | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Functional capability | 0–3 | 0 absent; 1 documented design; 2 implemented; 3 automated executable behavior |
| Evidence strength | 0–3 | 0 assertion only; 1 internal artifact; 2 reproducible hosted/clean-environment evidence; 3 independent external reproduction |
| Interoperability | 0–2 | 0 proprietary/unverified; 1 export/import or documented mapping; 2 executable standard/native integration |
| External use | 0–2 | 0 none established; 1 attributable external technical use/review; 2 accepted upstream, partner/customer use, or operational use in the bounded scope |

Maximum per dimension: **10**.

A total competitive score is valid only when every compared candidate has a non-`NOT_EVALUATED` result for every dimension in the declared score set.

## Benchmark dimensions

### A1 — Exact artifact / release identity

Must bind evaluated behavior to an immutable commit or artifact digest and preserve superseded evidence.

### A2 — Build provenance and attestation

Must produce verifiable provenance covering source/build identity. Native SLSA/in-toto/Sigstore/GitHub attestation compatibility receives stronger interoperability credit than a bespoke-only manifest.

### A3 — SBOM and dependency evidence

Must emit or ingest machine-readable dependency evidence and preserve exact version identity.

### A4 — Policy authorization / human authority

Must distinguish automated proposal from identified-human approval for consequential operations and fail closed when approval is absent.

### A5 — Decision evidence

Must retain the policy/authorization basis for a decision in a form that supports later audit and correlation.

### A6 — Tamper detection / integrity binding

Must detect alteration of protected inputs/evidence before treating them as valid.

### A7 — Replay / duplicate semantics

Must have deterministic and testable handling for duplicate/replayed inputs where replay is in scope.

### A8 — Recovery / rollback / restart evidence

Must demonstrate recovery/rollback behavior and retain evidence of configuration/state restoration.

### A9 — Discrepancy retention / negative evidence

Failed runs and falsifying evidence must remain attributable and must not be overwritten by a later passing run.

### A10 — External-evaluator independence

A benchmark can score maximum evidence only when an outside evaluator controls at least the declared challenge inputs/environment/timing/original evidence custody.

### A11 — Observability / trace correlation

Must support correlation from request/action through decision and evidence without relying solely on an agent/system self-report.

### A12 — Cross-domain evidence envelope

Must be capable of applying the same evidence/authority/custody semantics to more than software-supply-chain artifacts without auto-promoting scientific, physical, clinical, regulatory, or operational maturity.

### A13 — Standards-native exchange

Target native or executable compatibility with applicable public standards/formats. Initial targets:

- SLSA / in-toto attestations;
- Sigstore/Cosign verification where applicable;
- SPDX and/or CycloneDX SBOM;
- OPA/Rego policy exchange or decision correlation where useful;
- OCSF evidence/event mapping where the schema scope fits;
- OpenTelemetry trace context where useful for correlation.

### A14 — Offline / disconnected verification

Must support bounded verification without a live dependency on a Worldshepherd-operated service for cases where the evidence package is intended to be portable.

### A15 — Licensing / right-to-use clarity

The evaluator/customer must be able to determine what rights it has to execute, inspect, integrate, modify, or redistribute the delivered artifact. Public visibility alone does not score.

### A16 — External adoption / incorporation

Strongest credit requires evidence that an external organization actually used, incorporated, upstreamed, purchased, deployed, or selected the bounded capability. Outreach and meetings do not score.

## Composite-win condition

Worldshepherd may claim that a frozen release **outscored the declared comparison set on this benchmark** only when all of the following are true:

1. comparison candidates and exact versions are frozen;
2. every scored dimension is evaluated under the same protocol;
3. raw results and scoring inputs are public or available to the evaluator;
4. `NOT_EVALUATED` is not converted to zero;
5. Worldshepherd does not choose final external challenge inputs for independent-evidence points;
6. a result bundle records per-dimension scores, hashes, environment fingerprint and discrepancies;
7. at least one evaluator outside the Worldshepherd development process can reproduce the total;
8. the claim names the benchmark and date rather than implying universal superiority.

Example allowed statement after closure:

> `On Worldshepherd Assurance Composite Benchmark v0.1, frozen release <SHA> scored <X>/<Y> versus the tested comparison set <versions>, under the published protocol and external-evaluator record <artifact>.`

Not allowed:

> `Worldshepherd is better than every competitor.`

## Engineering strategy

The path to a legitimate high score is **composition plus missing assurance controls**:

1. use mature standards for provenance/signing/SBOM/policy/event semantics;
2. retain Worldshepherd's bounded human authority, evidence custody, replay/tamper, rollback/recovery and discrepancy controls;
3. make the combined path portable and independently reproducible;
4. minimize bespoke formats where a stable ecosystem format exists;
5. measure integration overhead, latency and failure behavior rather than relying only on feature presence;
6. require real outside use for the final adoption points.

This benchmark exists to make competitors useful: every capability they do better becomes a concrete Worldshepherd engineering requirement until Worldshepherd matches it, interoperates with it, or deliberately narrows scope with evidence.
