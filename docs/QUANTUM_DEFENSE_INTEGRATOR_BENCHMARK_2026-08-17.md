# Worldshepherd Quantum Defense-Integrator Benchmark

**Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Purpose:** Extend the quantum-market benchmark beyond QPU/platform vendors to mission integrators whose public positioning overlaps Worldshepherd more directly.

## Why this comparison matters

IBM, Google, Quantinuum, IonQ and D-Wave are primarily hardware/platform references. For Worldshepherd's intended role as a mission integration and evidence-governance layer, **Lockheed Martin and Leidos are more direct strategic comparators** because they combine quantum technologies with defense engineering, PNT, sensing, communications, data fusion, prototyping and field transition.

Worldshepherd should not claim parity with their scale, contracts, facilities, personnel, certification history or deployed systems. The useful benchmark is which integration disciplines Worldshepherd must demonstrate independently and where provider-neutral evidence governance could differentiate it.

## Lockheed Martin reference

Current public Lockheed Martin quantum activities include:

- a Quantum Innovation Center intended to accelerate quantum R&D and prototypes for critical/dual-use missions;
- focus areas spanning quantum remote sensing/communications, sensing/navigation/timing, computing and advanced quantum technologies;
- quantum computing work in chemistry, materials science and machine learning;
- DIU quantum-enabled inertial navigation prototyping (QuINS);
- DARPA Robust Quantum Sensors work with Q-CTRL;
- current public partnerships with Q-CTRL for navigation, PsiQuantum for quantum computing research/application, and Xanadu for quantum machine learning research.

### Worldshepherd use

Lockheed becomes a **mission-transition benchmark** for:

1. linking quantum R&D to named operational requirements;
2. integrating quantum navigation with conventional GPS/PNT rather than treating it as a standalone replacement;
3. partner-based hardware acquisition instead of requiring internal ownership of every modality;
4. prototyping under DoD/DIU/DARPA programs;
5. aerospace/defense environmental qualification and system integration.

Worldshepherd differentiation target: preserve a more explicit vendor-neutral evidence/claims chain across multiple competing providers and algorithms while still reaching comparable mission-integration discipline.

## Leidos reference

Current public Leidos quantum positioning emphasizes:

- precision sensing, secure communications and navigation in GPS-denied environments;
- mission-specific quantum use rather than technology for its own sake;
- calibration and data fusion as integration requirements;
- transition through DARPA, DIU and federal programs;
- DIU Transitioning Quantum Sensors work for resilient PNT;
- quantum-augmented networking;
- drone-based fusion of LiDAR and quantum gravimetry for infrastructure sensing;
- photonic/atomic RF sensing for compact wide-band mission systems.

### Worldshepherd use

Leidos becomes a **mission-engineering benchmark** for:

1. calibration and sensor-fusion discipline;
2. GPS-denied PNT integration;
3. secure communications/networking;
4. low-SWaP field sensing;
5. moving from research partnerships to mission-ready prototypes;
6. explicit focus on operational advantage rather than generic quantum claims.

Worldshepherd differentiation target: add transparent cross-provider comparator evidence, explicit negative-result retention, statistical reproduction, no-skip stage governance, and human-bound promotion review around those same mission-engineering disciplines.

## Defense-market parity gates for Worldshepherd

### Gate DQ-1 — credible external compute evidence

- one real IBM QPU run;
- same-backend repeat;
- second provider run;
- statistical reproduction under frozen canonical program;
- queue, latency, cost and failure telemetry retained.

### Gate DQ-2 — credible PNT evidence

- calibrated named quantum sensor/device/dataset;
- truth reference and quantified uncertainty;
- conventional INS/GPS comparator;
- denied/degraded GNSS replay;
- at least one live HIL path;
- environmental conditions retained.

### Gate DQ-3 — mission integration evidence

- conventional + quantum sensor fusion;
- explicit mission requirement and acceptance criteria;
- DDIL/degraded-state behavior;
- configuration custody and SBOM/provenance;
- human review and separate promotion authorization;
- relevant-environment demonstration.

### Gate DQ-4 — differentiated evidence governance

- same benchmark executed through multiple competing providers/modalities;
- vendor packages normalized into one evidence schema;
- incomplete package demonstrably rejected;
- statistically reproducible package accepted only after current-gate match;
- independent external reviewer evaluates methodology;
- public/vendor-neutral Quantum Mission Evidence Profile drafted after IP/legal review.

## Positioning conclusion

Worldshepherd's defensible defense-market ambition is **not** to claim it has surpassed Lockheed Martin or Leidos. Their public programs demonstrate far greater physical, contractual and field-integration maturity.

The attainable differentiated position is:

> **A small, provider-neutral quantum mission qualification architecture that can bring competing quantum compute and sensing resources into defense/aerospace workflows while retaining unusually explicit provenance, classical/null baselines, statistical reproduction, degraded-state evidence and human-governed claim promotion.**

If Worldshepherd cannot demonstrate DQ-1 through DQ-3 physically, the differentiation remains architectural. DQ-4 becomes meaningful only after those physical gates exist.
