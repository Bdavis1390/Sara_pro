# Gāndhārī as a Natural Experiment in Translation Drift

## Why this corpus matters

Gandhari.org reports thousands of documented corrections and improvements to previously published source texts.

The editors state that:
- published editions are critically re-evaluated;
- corrections are documented and justified;
- historical readings are preserved;
- accepted readings are progressively integrated with their interpretive history.

That creates exactly the evidence ATIP needs to separate:

**source correction**

from:

**meaning-changing translation correction.**

## The central experiment

For a source change:

old reading -> current reading

predict, before looking at the later interpretation:

- whether the change is material to meaning;
- whether a dependent translation should be re-audited;
- what kind of uncertainty is introduced.

Then reveal later/current interpretation and compare.

## Possible outcomes

### Source changed, translation stable
A spelling/sign-normalization correction does not alter meaning.

### Source changed, translation needs revision
A lexical, morphological, syntactic or named-entity correction changes the semantic dependency.

### Source changed, interpretation remains unresolved
The newer reading improves the text but does not eliminate competing interpretations.

### Interpretation changes without source reading change
New linguistic knowledge can alter translation even when the transcription remains stable.

This last outcome is crucial: translation drift is not caused only by witness changes.

## Research value

At sufficient scale, this can estimate:
- how often textual corrections materially alter meaning;
- which correction classes are most dangerous to downstream translations;
- which uncertainty signals predict later revision;
- how often older interpretations survive improved readings.

That turns translation verification from anecdotal error-finding into an empirical science of **translation dependency stability**.
