# Finding: Manuscripts Are Multiplex Information Carriers

## Beyond text

The translation program began by treating a manuscript as a textual witness.

That is insufficient.

A physical manuscript can simultaneously encode:
- linguistic information in ink;
- geometric information in fragment edges and fibre structure;
- biological information in parchment DNA/proteins;
- chemical information in pigments and ink;
- production history in preparation techniques;
- handling/conservation history in microbiomes and residues.

These channels can be partly independent.

## Dead Sea Scrolls: material evidence changes textual history

The 2020 Cell study on Dead Sea Scroll DNA is a decisive positive control.

Ancient DNA from parchment was used to genetically classify fragments. That evidence helped resolve debated fragment relationships and contributed to conclusions about:
- manuscript origin;
- circulation beyond Qumran;
- the parallel existence of divergent Jeremiah textual versions.

The critical lesson is:

**material provenance can falsify or refine a text-only reconstruction.**

A fragment that "looks right" textually may still belong to another physical object.

## 2026: this becomes scalable and nondestructive

Stinson and colleagues applied nondestructive genetic sampling to 91 parchment manuscripts.

That changes the operational barrier.

Material-genetic provenance no longer needs to remain a rare destructive experiment; it can become a routine optional evidence layer where curators permit it.

## Herculaneum: material chemistry can choose the next measurement

The September 2026 PLOS One work adds a second leap.

Researchers fabricated carbonized papyrus with known text and controlled lead concentrations.

They showed:
- handheld XRF can detect lead after carbonization;
- lead-containing hidden text can be recovered after tomography/virtual unwrapping;
- synthetic scrolls can supply ground truth.

The important systems insight is:

**evidence acquisition itself can be adaptive.**

Instead of scanning every object identically, first ask what the material tells us about which sensor is likely to work.

## New chain

artifact
-> cheap material characterization
-> sensing-plan selection
-> high-resolution acquisition
-> physical reconstruction
-> textual reconstruction
-> translation.

This is active scientific perception rather than passive digitization.

## The Worldshepherd principle

A generally capable evidence system should not ask only:

"What does the observed representation say?"

It should also ask:

"What other independent physical channels does this object contain, and which measurement should I perform next to reduce uncertainty?"

That is the bridge from static interpretation to active epistemic agency.
