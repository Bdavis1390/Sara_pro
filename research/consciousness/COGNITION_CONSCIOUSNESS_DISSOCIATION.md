# Cognition–Consciousness Dissociation

## 1. The key finding

The most important implication of Katlowitz et al. 2026 is not merely that the anesthetized brain remains active.

It is that **some surprisingly sophisticated computations persist despite anesthesia-induced loss of consciousness**.

Direct hippocampal recordings showed:
- oddball discrimination;
- representational plasticity over minutes;
- semantic feature coding;
- grammatical feature coding;
- predictive information about upcoming words.

Therefore several operations that look "intelligent" are not sufficient markers of consciousness.

## 2. A new decomposition

Worldshepherd should separate at least four axes:

### Processing capacity
Can the system transform complex inputs?

### Semantic competence
Can it represent relationships, grammar, meaning and context?

### Agency
Can it select actions that causally alter future states?

### Consciousness
Is there subjective experience / conscious access?

These axes may correlate, but they are not interchangeable.

## 3. Why this matters for AGI

Language models can display:
- semantic abstraction;
- syntactic competence;
- prediction;
- contextual learning;
- planning-like behavior.

The human neuroscience result shows that analogous classes of computation can occur in a brain state operationally classified as unconscious.

Therefore:

**linguistic sophistication cannot by itself serve as evidence that an artificial system is conscious.**

This is a conceptual safety improvement.

It avoids two opposite errors:

1. anthropomorphism:
   "it understands language, therefore it must be conscious";

2. premature dismissal:
   "it lacks human-like report, therefore no relevant internal cognition exists."

Instead, evaluate each property independently.

## 4. The connectome result points toward a possible missing layer

Zhu et al. 2026 examined 117 patients with disorders of consciousness and 30 healthy controls.

They found:
- widespread loss of functional integration and segregation;
- segregation deficits were especially widespread across pathological states;
- residual integration correlated with behavioral consciousness;
- network topology contributed to prognosis;
- adding clinical variables increased PR-AUC to 0.84.

This motivates a layered hypothesis:

local computation can survive while global integration/access is impaired.

Formally:

local semantic competence != global conscious access.

## 5. Candidate architecture

Let:
- L_i(t) = local/subsystem computation;
- S_i(t) = semantic representations;
- G(t) = large-scale integration;
- Q(t) = segregation/specialization;
- D(t) = directional routing;
- M(t) = metastable switching;
- R(t) = report/action access;
- C(t) = conscious-state variable.

Then test:

C(t) = F(G,Q,D,M,...)

while allowing:

S_i(t) > 0

even when:

C(t) is low/absent under the operational state definition.

## 6. Strong falsification

The global-access hypothesis weakens if:
- local semantic processing reliably disappears whenever consciousness is absent;
- large-scale integration has no incremental association with conscious behavior after confounds;
- integration/segregation metrics fail replication;
- conscious experience can be demonstrated with permanently absent global routing.

## 7. Relation to the original frequency lesson

This further reduces the usefulness of single-frequency explanations.

A local circuit can remain computationally sophisticated while the large-scale system loses conscious organization.

Therefore the relevant variable is likely closer to:

**coordinated topology + directional flow + timing + state transitions**

than to:

**presence of semantic processing or one oscillatory band**.

## 8. Worldshepherd AGI rule

Never infer consciousness from:
- fluent language;
- semantic prediction;
- memory-like behavior;
- local learning;
- successful task completion alone.

Maintain separate evidence ledgers for:
- capability;
- agency;
- self-model;
- global integration;
- consciousness-specific evidence.

This keeps capability assessment from silently becoming a consciousness claim.
